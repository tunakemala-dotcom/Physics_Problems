# Task 10 – Light Speed Measurement

## Problem Statement

Measure the speed of light using a microwave oven ($f = 2.45\text{ GHz}$) and chocolate/cheese. Measure the distance between melted spots to determine $\lambda$. Calculate $c$, compare to accepted value, and find percentage error.

## Theory

A microwave oven operates by generating a standing electromagnetic wave inside the cooking chamber. The antinodes (points of maximum amplitude) transfer the most thermal energy, melting the chocolate at these specific locations. 
The distance between two adjacent antinodes in a standing wave is exactly half a wavelength ($\lambda/2$).

$$
\text{Distance} = \frac{\lambda}{2} \implies \lambda = 2 \times \text{Distance}
$$

The speed of the wave is:

$$
c = f \lambda
$$

## Step-by-Step Solution

### 1. Assumed Experimental Data

Suppose the measured distance between adjacent melted spots on the chocolate bar is $d = 6.0\text{ cm} = 0.060\text{ m}$.

### 2. Calculate Wavelength

$$
\lambda = 2d = 2(0.060\text{ m}) = 0.120\text{ m}
$$

### 3. Calculate Speed of Light

Given frequency $f = 2.45\text{ GHz} = 2.45 \times 10^9\text{ Hz}$:

$$
c_{measured} = (2.45 \times 10^9\text{ Hz})(0.120\text{ m})
$$

$$
c_{measured} = 2.94 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}
$$

### 4. Comparison and Percentage Error

The accepted value is $c \approx 3.00 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = \left| \frac{c_{measured} - c_{accepted}}{c_{accepted}} \right| \times 100\%
$$

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = \left| \frac{2.94 \times 10^8 - 3.00 \times 10^8}{3.00 \times 10^8} \right| \times 100\%
$$

$$
\text{Percentage Error} = \left| \frac{-0.06}{3.00} \right| \times 100\% = 2.0\%
$$

## Final Result

* Calculated speed of light: $2.94 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$
* Percentage error: $2.0\%$

## Interpretation

This kitchen experiment yields surprisingly accurate results. The primary source of error is the physical measurement of the melt spots with a standard ruler, as the melted regions tend to be wide and smeared rather than precise geometric points.
